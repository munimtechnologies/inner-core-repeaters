# Diagnostic plan (exploratory): why YKA's calibration failed, and what splits the ILAR BC−DF times

MV2, Munim, Inc. (made with AI agents). Written 2026-11-18, after reading the results of
`icr-ykab-v2` and `icr-bcdf-v2` ([results](2026-11-18-ykab-v2-bcdf-v2-results.md)) and before

**Status: exploratory.** Nothing here changes a pre-registered verdict. Pre-registration 8
said that if P4-Y fails, nothing at YKA is interpreted, and that stands. This note exists
so the diagnostics cannot be tuned after their outputs are seen, and so the next confirmatory
step can be designed on a selection rule that was fixed in advance.

## Part Y: why is P4-Y failing at YKA?

P4-Y compares pairs less than 2 years apart ("near") with pairs that are more than 10 years
apart and more than 5 years apart on the backtracking clock ("far"). In v2 the near median D
(0.098) was *higher* than the far median (0.084). Four explanations, each with a check that
`icrdiag.py` runs on the published v2 pair table (`results/ykab-v2/yka_pairs.csv`) plus USGS
ComCat locations:

| Explanation | Check | What would support it |
|---|---|---|
| **Simpson's paradox.** Near pairs come mostly from a few families with a high baseline D (the 2021-22 M8.1 sequence); far pairs come from quieter families. | `within_family`: sign test over families with ≥ 2 near and ≥ 2 far pairs; P4 on D minus the family median | most families have near < far (sign-test p < 0.05), and family-demeaned P4 holds |
| **The 2021-22 sequence.** Its aftershocks are spread along the rupture, so "repeaters" from it are not true repeaters. | P4 without pairs touching 2021.6-2023.0; near-pair share from that sequence | P4 holds without it; most near pairs come from it |
| **Noise.** D is set by the weaker event's SNR. | rank regression of D on Δt, separation, depth difference, min SNR, Δmag and the 2021 flag (family bootstrap); P4 in the top half by SNR | the min-SNR coefficient dominates; P4 holds at high SNR |
| **Location.** Families defined at ILAR mix sources tens of km apart. | the same regression; P4 in the closer half of pairs | the separation coefficient is positive and its 5-95% range excludes 0 |

All of this is computed for D (the pre-registered +1 to +6 s window) and for the two
sensitivity windows. D_c13 already passed P4 in v2 (0.044 < 0.047) and is reported, not
promoted.

**What happens next, fixed now.** If one of the explanations is supported, the next
confirmatory YKA test will use a selection rule built from it (for example "within-family
calibration" or "both events with direct SNR ≥ X"). It will be applied **only to South
Sandwich Islands events that occur after this note is published** (a prospective test), because
every 2025-2026 YKA pair has now been seen. If none is supported, YKA coda similarity at
M4.3-5.3 is written up as too noisy for this question and the YKA lane is closed.

## Part B: two branches in the ILAR BC−DF residuals

After the spatial terms, the BC−DF residuals in `icr-bcdf-v2` fall into two groups about
0.6 s apart (see `results/bcdf-v2/bcdf_vs_time.png`). That is close to one period of the
1-2 Hz band, so the BC correlation may lock onto the wrong cycle. It could also be real
geography: two parts of the arc whose rays cross the Alaska slab differently.

`icrdiag.py` fits a two-Gaussian mixture to the residuals and labels each event's branch.
It then checks:

* **Geography:** does an event's nearest catalogue neighbour share its branch more often than
  in 1,000 shuffles (1 and 5 neighbours)? Are latitude, longitude or depth different
  between the branches?
* **Measurement:** do the branches differ in DF onset, correlation or DF SNR? Does the
  element-median measurement put the event on the same branch?
* **Time:** the share of upper-branch events before 2008.5, 2008.5-2021.6, 2021.6-2025 and
  from 2025, and the slope of each branch with its standard error.

| Result | Reading |
|---|---|
| neighbour agreement above the 95% shuffle level, and lat/lon differ | geography: model it (a branch or patch term), not a picking fix |
| agreement at shuffle level, with the branches differing in cc or SNR | cycle skipping: fix the BC pick (envelope-guided or first-swing correlation) and re-run as a new pre-registered version |
| the upper-branch share changes with time | branch switching would look like a time signal; any trend must then be measured within a branch |

The lower-branch slope and its standard error give the precision the ILAR BC−DF series can
reach. It is reported as a limit, not a test.

## Run

USGS catalogue, about 3 MB), then `python -m unittest -v test_icrdiag`. 1 CPU, 1 GB.
