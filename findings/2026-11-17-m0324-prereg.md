# Pre-registration: the M03-24 patch test (ILAR, 1991-2026)

MV2, Munim, Inc. (made with AI agents). Written 2026-11-17, before any waveform from this
patch was downloaded or processed by this project. Code: `m0324.py` (v0.5.0), reusing the
ILAR pipeline in `run.py` unchanged. Text CC BY 4.0, code Apache-2.0.

## Why this patch

Two 2026 papers read the same South Sandwich Islands (SSI) -> Alaska data differently:

* **Wang, Vidale et al. 2024** (*Nature* 631:340) and **Vidale et al. 2025** (*Nat. Geosci.*):
  the inner core stopped and turned back around **2008.5** and is retracing its path about
  **2.5x** more slowly. An event at time t > 2008.5 sits where the inner core was at the
  equivalent date e(t) = 2008.5 - (t - 2008.5)/2.5. For **2025.0-2026.0, e(t) =
  2001.9-2001.5**.
* **Zhang & Wen 2026** (*JGR Solid Earth* 131, e2026JB034595; data CC BY 4.0 at
  doi:10.5281/zenodo.19625711) use one SSI multiplet, **M03-24**, recorded at ILAR and
  YKA, to map inner-core *surface* change. They report irregular episodes of change,
  quiescence and recovery that need no rotation. Their six events (identified from the
  Zenodo file tree and matched to USGS ComCat on 2026-11-17):

| usp000fz4d | 2008-02-17 19:32:09.1 | -58.986 | -25.697 | 66.5 | 4.9 |

| us6000mk65 | 2024-03-21 02:47:21.9 | -59.012 | -25.637 | 43.9 | 5.2 |

Centroid: about **(-59.036, -25.694)**.

What we found on 2026-11-17 (USGS ComCat, before any waveform):

* **2025-2026 is very active here.** 46 events of M >= 4.3 fall within 40 km of the
  centroid between 2025-01-01 and 2026-11-13. Examples: 2025-03-26 Mww 5.3,
  2025-10-21 mb 5.2, 2025-12-15 mb 5.3, 2026-11-12 mb 5.4. No published study uses any of
  them: all inner-core papers to date stop in 2024.
* **The patch has events at the backtracking forecast's dates.** Within 25 km of the
  centroid there are 2001-01-29 (mb 4.8) and 2002-06-29 (mb 4.5). Under Wang et al.'s
  model these are the partners a 2025-2026 event should match best.
* **The main ILAR run misses the key partners.** `icr-ilar-v1` (`run.py`) uses M >= 5.0
  before 2024-07 and M >= 4.7 after. It therefore drops:
  * two of the six M03-24 members (2003, mb 4.8; 2008, mb 4.9);
  * both 2001-2002 partners;
  * 18 of the 46 events from 2025-2026.

This makes M03-24 the one place where an independent group's mapped multiplet and a dense
set of new events coincide. It also sits next to partners at exactly the forecast dates.

## Data access, checked 2026-11-17 (no waveforms analysed)

Small requests to the EarthScope FDSN dataselect service returned miniSEED, not "no data", for:

* IM.IL01 SHZ around PKIKP of 2026-07-11 (M6.4) and 2002-03-09 (M6.0).
* DW.COL SHZ around PKIKP of 1985-05-15, the 1980s College station used by `cola.py`.
* IU.COLA 00 BHZ (2010).

IM.IL01 SHZ around the 1990-05-09 event returned **no data**, and so did a random 1993
window. ILAR coverage before about 1995 may therefore be sparse; the job reports coverage
by year.

Service changes affect these tools. EarthScope retired fdsnws-event (the code uses USGS
ComCat instead) and irisws-timeseries. The fdsnws-availability service has been offline
since July 2026, so coverage can only be learned by requesting the data.

## Method (fixed)

1. **Events.** All USGS events of M >= 4.3 within 35 km of the centroid, 1991-01-01 to the
   run date, plus the six M03-24 members whatever their magnitude or distance.
2. **Waveforms and beams.** Each event goes through `run.py` exactly as in the main
   pre-registration (findings/2026-11-17-prereg.md):
   * ILAR IL01-IL19 SHZ, 1-2 Hz causal band-pass, cut on ak135 PKIKP;
   * per-event statics from the PKP group, then a beam;
   * repeater edges where the PKPbc+PKPab reference window correlates >= 0.90;
   * multiplets as connected components;
   * DF dissimilarity D = 1 - cc in the DF window after reference alignment;
   * a pair is "ok" if cc_ref >= 0.93, reference SNR >= 5 and DF SNR >= 3.

## Tests and decision rules

* **Z0, reproduction of M03-24.** Holds if >= 4 of the six published members are processed
  and >= 4 (and >= 2/3 of those processed) fall in one of our multiplets. This checks an
  independent group's multiplet, found by eye, against our objective pipeline.
* **Z1, new-event best match (main test).** This is `model.p3` on the patch's ok pairs. It
  uses each 2025+ event with >= 2 pre-2008.5 partners where the backtracking pick (partner
  closest to e(t), so about 2001-2002) differs from the monotone pick (the latest pre-turn
  partner).
  * Testable if there are >= 2 such events.
  * **Backtracking supported** if more of them best-match the B pick than the M pick.
  * **Backtracking contradicted** if more best-match the M pick.
  * A tie is inconclusive.
* **Z2, out-of-sample rank test.** This is `model.grade_set` on the patch's ok pairs with a
  2025+ member: Spearman of D against |e(t1) - e(t2)| is positive with p < 0.05 and beats
  |t2 - t1| in >= 90% of multiplet bootstraps. It needs >= 6 pairs and >= 3 that cross the
  turn.
* **Z3, calibration.** This is `model.p4`: pairs < 2 years apart have lower median D than
  pairs > 10 years apart under both models. If Z3 fails, Z1 and Z2 are reported but not
  interpreted.
* **Z5, exploratory.** The best-fitting (turn time, rate ratio); this is not a test.

The alternative is reported, not tested. Zhang & Wen's surface-change view predicts no
systematic preference for the e(t) partner. A clean Z1 "B" result at this patch is evidence
for the rotation reading at the very location they used against it. A clean "M" result,
or no preference, counts against the 2024 forecast. Either way the result will be
published.

## Forecasts (written before the data)

* Z0 holds: **55%**. There is risk from mb 4.8-4.9 SNR at 1-2 Hz and from 2003 data gaps.
* Z1 is testable at all (>= 2 discriminating 2025-26 events with ok pairs to pre-2008.5
  partners): **40%**.
* If testable, the outcome:

  | B wins | M wins | tie |
  |---|---|---|
  | 45% | 25% | 30% |

* Z3 holds if testable: **75%**.

## Honest limits

* The patch is about 30 km across, and USGS locations scatter by about 10 km. Repeaters are
  defined by waveform, not by catalogue location.
* Many pre-2005 ILAR records may be missing.
* A single patch samples one small region of the inner-core surface beneath South America.
  It cannot by itself separate rigid rotation from local surface change. It tests one
  sharp, dated forecast at one place where both readings have staked a claim.
