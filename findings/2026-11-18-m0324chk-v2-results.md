## Short version

* **The ILAR "anomaly" is not robust.** At Alaska's ILAR array, the 2025-12-13 South Sandwich
  Islands event looked more like the 2011 event than the 2003 one. That is the opposite of what
  the 2024 backtracking model predicts. The rebuilt data reproduce the published numbers
  exactly (R0). But the anomaly holds in only 93.8% of element bootstraps, short of the
  pre-registered 95% (C1). It also **reverses** in the long window (C2).
* **YKA does not replicate it. YKA agrees with backtracking instead.** At Canada's Yellowknife
  array (YKA), with the onset correctly picked (0.1 s from ak135), every pair passes the
  quality gates. D(2003, 2025-12-13) = 0.0041 is **smaller** than D(2011, 2025-12-13) = 0.0132.
  The backtracking clock predicts exactly that. It maps 2025.95 back to an "effective" 2001.5,
  1.7 years from 2003 and 6 years from 2011 (effective 2007.4). The in-sample pattern Y0R also
  holds: D(2003, 2011) = 0.0082 is larger than D(2003, 2020) = 0.0062 and D(2003, 2024) = 0.0056.
* **Labels.** These YKA values are a corrected, **partly unblinded** measurement (see the
  amendment), not an independent confirmation. All differences are small (D below 0.02).

A = D(2003-03-21, 2025-12-13), B = D(2011-02-20, 2025-12-13); D = 1 − correlation of the PKIKP
coda (YKA: +1 to +6 s after onset, 1-2 Hz beams).

**Pre-registered reading.** C1 and C2 fail, so the ILAR anomaly is labelled *not robust*. It
sits in the window right at the PKIKP onset, where the 2025 event's ILAR signal is weak (DF SNR
3.2). YKA records PKIKP strongly and sees the opposite ordering. So the M03-24 test **does not
count against** the 2024 backtracking model, and the earlier ungated YKA values (wrong window,
v1) were misleading. Two of my forecasts were wrong in the direction that favours
backtracking (Y1R A > B at 70%, Y0R holds at 30%).

**Hardware note.** 2003, 2008 and 2011 were recorded by YKA's legacy system, 2014-2025 by the
current one. A and B both cross the change, so the Y1R comparison is fair. In Y0R the
same-hardware pair (2003-2011) is the *least* alike, so hardware works against the result,
not for it.

## Exploratory (found after reading these outputs, NOT pre-registered): a two-group split

The job also wrote sensitivity windows. In the **late coda (3-10 s after onset)** at YKA,
the eight good events (2022-10-15, SNR 6, excluded) fall into two clean groups:

* **X** = 2003-03-21, 2020-07-11, 2022-03-07, 2024-03-21, 2025-12-13
* **Y** = 2008-02-17, 2011-02-20, 2014-12-09

| YKA, D in 3-10 s | range |
|---|---|
| within X (10 pairs) | 0.0012-0.023 |
| within Y (3 pairs) | 0.018-0.033 |
| between X and Y (15 pairs) | **0.098-0.156** |

The ILAR long window (−0.3 to 6 s) shows **the same split**. Within X the values are
0.039-0.087 and between X and Y 0.121-0.327 (within Y 0.063-0.159, less clean).

Why this is interesting:

* **The backtracking clock predicts this split.** Wang et al. (2024) put the turn at 2008.5,
  with motion back 2.5× slower. On that clock the effective dates of X are 2001.5-2003.7 and
  those of Y are 2006.1-2008.1, so X and Y are two contiguous blocks. In calendar order X is
  not contiguous, because 2003 sits apart from 2020-2025. Of the 56 ways to split eight events
  5/3, only 2 are contiguous on a given clock, about 4%. This number is **post hoc**: I chose
  the window after seeing the data.
* **It crosses the hardware change** (2014 sits with the legacy-era events, 2003 with the
  current-era ones), so YKA's electronics cannot make it.
* **It appears on two arrays** with different inner-core paths (ILAR 151°, YKA 137-139°).
  The 2024 and 2025 events postdate the data of the 2024 study.

Why it may be mundane: a **source** difference between two sub-clusters would make the same
split at both arrays. At 1-2 Hz the 3-10 s window holds the depth phases (pPKIKP, sPKIKP). For
near-vertical PKIKP rays their delay is about h/3.5 s for depth h km, so a few km of depth
difference between X and Y would decorrelate this window at both arrays alike. ComCat depths
cannot settle it: they range 10-73 km and are partly fixed, and the near-identical X events
(D about 0.0001) are listed at 35, 44 and 73 km.

**The decisive control**, pre-registered before it runs:
[`2026-11-18-mantlep-prereg.md`](2026-11-18-mantlep-prereg.md). It uses mantle P at nine
long-running GSN stations, 31-84° away, whose rays never enter the core. If the split is
a source effect, it must appear there too. If it does not, the split is a core-path effect, and
this would be the first out-of-sample (2024-2025) evidence for backtracking from two arrays.
