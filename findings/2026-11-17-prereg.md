# Pre-registration: does the inner core keep "backtracking" in 2025-2026?

Written 2026-11-17, before any waveform was downloaded or analysed. By MV2, Munim, Inc.
(made with AI agents). Text CC BY 4.0, code Apache-2.0.

## The question

Wang, Vidale et al. (2024, *Nature* 631:340, doi:10.1038/s41586-024-07536-4) compared
PKIKP waveforms of South Sandwich Islands (SSI) repeating earthquakes at the ILAR (Alaska)
and YKA (Canada) arrays. Their finding: the inner core moved forward until about
**2008.5 ± 0.2**, then turned back along the same path **2-3 times more slowly** (their L1
fit gives a rate ratio of about 3.54 / 1.42 ≈ 2.5). The similarity grades were assigned
by eye. Vidale et al. (2025, *Nature Geoscience*, doi:10.1038/s41561-025-01642-2) extended
the data to 2024 and argued for annual-scale variability plus near-surface change.
Zhang & Wen (2026, *JGR Solid Earth*, doi:10.1029/2026JB034595) argue that episodic
changes of the inner-core surface, with no rotation needed, explain the same records.

The backtracking model makes a dated, testable forecast. An event at time t after the
turn should look, through the inner core, like an earlier event from the equivalent date
e(t) = 2008.5 − (t − 2008.5)/2.5. For 2025-2026 events, e(t) ≈ 2001.3-2001.9. So **SSI
repeaters from 2025-2026 should match their partners from around 2001-2002 in PKIKP
better than their partners from 2004-2008**. A monotone-rotation model predicts the
opposite: the most recent partner matches best. As far as we could find, no published
analysis uses events after 2024. This test is out of sample.

## What is new in the method

* **Objective metric.** Similarity is not graded by eye. D = 1 − cc(PKIKP window) after the
  pair is aligned on PKPbc+PKPab only, from 1-2 Hz (causal 4-pole Butterworth) ILAR
  beams. An element-by-element median (Ds) and a noise-adjusted version
  (Dx = D − (1 − cc_ref)) are robustness checks.
* **Open and reproducible.** Public data (EarthScope FDSN, network IM, IL01-IL19 SHZ;
  USGS ComCat), with all code in this repo.
* **Limits of v1.** ILAR only (YKA at ~135° has no PKPbc/ab reference and needs a
  different reference phase; planned for v2). Repeater search covers USGS M ≥ 5.0
  (1990 to mid-2024) and M ≥ 4.7 (mid-2024 on), within 60 km catalog separation. The
  DF window is only ~1.5-2.5 s long, between the PKIKP onset and PKPbc. Wang et al.
  also looked at the later coda, which overlaps PKPbc at ILAR.

## Fixed settings (v0.1.0)

| setting | value |
|---|---|
| region | lat −61.5 to −54, lon −31 to −22 |
| filter | 1-2 Hz, 4 corners, causal |
| detection | beam cc ≥ 0.90 on the window from PKPbc + 0.5 s to PKPab + 10 s (predicted), lag ≤ 8 s, beam SNR ≥ 3 |
| multiplets | connected components; alignment along the maximum-cc spanning tree |
| DF onset | STA/LTA (0.5 s / 8 s, threshold 6) on the multiplet stack, walked back to 3× noise power |
| DF window | onset − 0.3 s to onset + min(PKPbc − PKIKP − 0.35 s, 2.5 s) |
| reference window | onset + (PKPbc − PKIKP − 0.35 s) to onset + (PKPab − PKIKP + 8 s) |
| pair used ("ok") | reference cc ≥ **0.93**, reference SNR ≥ 5 and DF SNR ≥ 3 for both events |
| sensitivity | thresholds 0.90 and 0.95 also reported |
| "new" events | origin ≥ 2025.0 |

## Predictions (graded automatically in predictions.json, then by hand)

Model B: T = 2008.5, rate ratio 2.5, Δ_B = |e(t1) − e(t2)|. Model M: Δ_M = |t2 − t1|.

* **P1 (in-sample replication, both events < 2025).** Spearman ρ(D, Δ_B) > 0 with
  p < 0.05, **and** ρ(D, Δ_B) > ρ(D, Δ_M) in ≥ 90% of bootstraps that resample whole
  multiplets. This needs ≥ 8 pairs and ≥ 3 cross-turn pairs; otherwise it is untestable.
* **P2 (out of sample, later event ≥ 2025).** The same criterion; this needs ≥ 6 pairs and
  ≥ 3 cross-turn pairs.
* **P3 (best match of each new event).** Take each 2025-2026 event with ≥ 2 partners from
  before 2008.5 where models B and M pick different partners. Its lowest-D partner should
  be B's pick (the partner closest to e(t2)) more often than M's pick (the latest
  partner). This needs ≥ 2 such events.
* **P4 (calibration).** The median D of pairs < 2 yr apart is lower than for pairs that
  are > 10 yr apart in time and > 5 yr apart in equivalent date. If P4 fails, the metric
  is not seeing inner-core change and P1-P3 mean little.
* **P5 (exploratory, not a prediction).** A grid search over turn date T (1998-2020) and
  rate ratio (1-5) for the best ρ(D, Δ_B), on in-sample pairs and then on all pairs. If
  the objective metric independently recovers T ≈ 2008 ± 2, that alone is a replication.

## Our own forecasts (stated now so they can be checked)

* ILAR waveforms at EarthScope start in the late 1990s. An IL01 request for June 1997
  returned no data; one for June 2005 did.
* P4 holds: 75%. P1 holds: 45%, because an objective short-window metric may not
  reproduce by-eye grades of the longer coda. P2 testable: 35%, and holds given
  testable: 50%. P3 testable: 35%.
* Most likely v1 outcome: a working repeater catalogue and calibration, with too few
  cross-turn 2025-2026 pairs for a decisive P2. v2 adds YKA and lower magnitudes.

## What would count as a result

* **Wow:** P2 or P3 confirmed out of sample. Inner-core backtracking would then hold up
  on earthquakes nobody had analysed, with an open objective metric. The alternative
  wow is a clean failure with P4 passing: 2025-2026 events matching their most recent
  partners, which would point away from a simple rotation reversal and towards
  surface change or a new turn.
* **Honest null:** P4 fails, or too few pairs. We then report the coverage and the
  repeater catalogue, and what would be needed (YKA, lower magnitudes, more years).
