## The question

Wang, Vidale et al. (2024, *Nature* 631:340) concluded that Earth's inner core reversed its
motion relative to the mantle around 2008.5 and has been retracing its path about 2.5 times
more slowly. If that continued, an earthquake in late 2026 sees the inner core about where it
was in early 2001, and its waves through the inner core should look most like those of a
repeating earthquake from about 2001. If the inner core has kept changing in one direction,
or its surface has been quiet since the early 2020s (Zhang & Wen 2026, *JGR*), the 2026 event
should look most like the most recent repeat instead.

Our ILAR pipeline links the 2026-11-13 South Sandwich Islands earthquake us7000ty3q (mb 5.1)
to six earlier events (multiplet Z006). For this family the two pictures pick **different**
partners:

| Partner | Years apart | Effective years apart (backtracking) | Picked by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2000-10-08 usp000a1fh (mb 4.3) | 26.10 | **0.38** | backtracking (B) |
| 2023-10-15 us6000lfpq (mb 4.8) | **3.08** | 1.23 | plain time order (M) |
| 2021-09-04, 2019-05-01, 2012-08-23, 2007-11-21 | 5.2-19.0 | 2.1-6.7 | neither |

No published study can have used the 2026 event. This is an out-of-sample test of the 2024
clock with an event two years after the paper, at an array (YKA, Canada) whose path differs
from ILAR's everywhere except near the source and inside the inner core.

## Method (code: `m27.py` 0.1.0, compete job `icr-m27-v1`)

**Part Y, core (P15-1, primary).** `m0324_check.run_yka` (v0.8.0, pinned) with `yka_repick`'s
±2 s onset picker, exactly as in icr-m26-v1, on the seven Z006 events. Gates unchanged:
reference cc >= 0.90, direct SNR >= 5, coda SNR >= 2 (column `ok`). The metric is `D`, yka.py's
measured PKIKP coda window (1-6 s after onset, 1 - correlation). The key contrast is

K = D(us7000ty3q, 2023-10-15) / D(us7000ty3q, 2000-10-08), both pairs `ok`.

* **B** if K >= 1.5; **M** if K <= 1/1.5; **inconclusive** otherwise;
* **untestable** if either pair is not `ok`.

**Part M, source control (P15-0).** `mantlep2.py` v0.13.0 (pinned commit b4712f0e), with its
event list replaced by the seven Z006 events, at the eight stations of pre-registration 13
(IU.QSPA, GT.VNDA, IU.LCO, GT.DBIC, GT.PLCA, GT.LPAZ, GT.LBTB, IU.LSZ). Same gates (direct SNR
>= 5, late SNR >= 2). At each station where the 2026, 2000 and 2023 events are all gated,
Km = D_late(2026, 2023) / D_late(2026, 2000) for mantle P 3-10 s after onset. This asks whether
the 2026 source is itself more like one partner than the other, since source location and
depth also change the coda.

**Headline.**
* **B-supported**: core B and median Km < 1.5 over >= 2 stations;
* **M-supported**: core M and median Km > 1/1.5 over >= 2 stations;
* **source-confounded:B / :M**: the core label, but mantle P favours the same partner by the
  same factor (median Km >= 1.5 for B, <= 1/1.5 for M);
* **source-unverified:B / :M**: fewer than 2 counted stations;
* **inconclusive** / **untestable** from P15-1.

**Secondaries (reported, not decisive).**
* S15a: Spearman of D(2026, x) against the backtracking distance (rho_B) and the plain time
  distance (rho_M) over all `ok` partners (needs >= 4), with the best-matching partner. Same
  for `D_c310` (3-10 s) and `D_c13` (1-3 s), and ungated.
* S15b: the contrast K for `D_c310` and `D_c13`, and ungated.
* S15c: family membership at mantle P: q = share of gated old events whose median D_late to
  the others is below the 2026 event's (as in pre-registration 14; >= 3 old events gated).
* S15d: Z007 (us6000ty0j, 2026-09-27, partners 2005-2022) and Z009 (us7000txqr, 2026-11-12,
  partners 2010 and 2022) at YKA, reported only. In both, the backtracking and time-order
  picks are the same event, so they cannot separate the pictures.

## What happens next

* **B-supported** would be the first out-of-sample support for the backtracking clock with a
  post-publication event. Before anyone is told, we check the YKA plots, the per-element
  values, the mantle-P plots and whether the 2000 event's YKA record (legacy array, 20 sps)
  differs in hardware from the 2023 and 2026 records. A hardware difference would bias
  toward M, not B.
* **M-supported** would be evidence against continued backtracking at the 2.5x rate. It would
  fit steady change or a quiet surface since 2023, but would not tell those two apart.
* If YKA has no 2026 data yet, the same code is re-run unchanged under a new job version.

## Limits written in advance

* One event and one key contrast at one array: a single test, not a population result.
* The family is defined by our ILAR pipeline (single linkage at cc >= 0.90 on PKPbc+ab), not
  by a published study. The catalogue depths of the 2000 and 2023 events are fixed at 33 and
  35 km.
* The ILAR values for this family are already known (they lean B, at low SNR). The YKA test
  does not use them, but our forecasts were written knowing them.
* The 1.5 ratio is a judgement, not calibrated on this family.
